Why should we care?

Because a system can appear highly accurate overall while failing drastically for a specific demographic group. The consequences of these errors depend entirely on the operational context:

● Lower-Stakes Context: Consumer Apps

In photo gallery sorting or playful social media filters, an incorrect gender tag causes mild user annoyance or awkwardness. The harm is generally low.

● High-Stakes Context: Security & Policing

In automated surveillance, border control checkpoints, or building security access, an error rate of 1 in 3 or 1 in 5 leads to wrongful detentions, denied entry, or harassment.

Buolamwini, J. & Gebru, T. (2018). "Gender Shades: Intersectional Accuracy Disparities in Commercial Gender Classification." Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 81:1–15.

How data is used in this application

The subgroup error rates shown in the comparison stage (Stage 03) are reproduced directly from the Gender Shades paper and are clearly labelled as research data.

All interactive experiments in Stage 04 — including the threshold slider, dataset balance toggle, human review scenario, and deployment decision — use illustrative, simulated values. These values are designed to demonstrate fairness concepts and should not be interpreted as empirical findings from the paper or from any real evaluation.

Test case responses in Stage 02 use pre-stored demo responses in the default Demo Mode. No real API calls are made unless Live Mode is explicitly enabled by the course instructor.

What this application does not claim

  • It does not claim any AI system is objectively "fair" or "unfair" based on one experiment.
  • It does not perform demographic inference on any user.
  • Model disagreement on individual test cases is not presented as evidence of bias.
  • Illustrative simulation values are never presented as research findings.

About the PPB dataset

The Pilot Parliaments Benchmark (PPB) was created by Joy Buolamwini as part of the Gender Shades research. It consists of photographs of members of parliament from three African countries and three European countries, selected to ensure roughly equal gender balance within two skin tone categories.

University Seminar Context

About this seminar & research

Seminar context

Built as an interactive, hands-on learning tool for university students and seminar participants exploring AI Ethics and Algorithmic Fairness. This lab allows students to act as AI auditors, critically examining commercial facial classification systems to understand how aggregate accuracy metrics can obscure acute demographic disparities.

Three types of data

  • 1. Real Gender Shades Research Results: The landmark peer-reviewed error rates from Joy Buolamwini and Timnit Gebru (2018) for Microsoft, Face++, and IBM across 4 intersectional subgroups (lighter male, lighter female, darker male, darker female).
  • 2. Live Model Results: Live predictions, confidence scores, and latency metrics fetched in real-time from the connected backend inference runner when LIVE mode is enabled.
  • 3. Illustrative Simulation: Carefully designed synthetic demonstration data for exploring decision thresholds, training set representation, and human-in-the-loop review policies (clearly labeled: "Illustrative simulation — not data from Gender Shades").

Privacy

No personal photographs, biometric templates, or individual personal identifiers are gathered or processed. Benchmark evaluations use controlled schematic test cases.

Research source

Buolamwini, J., & Gebru, T. (2018). Gender Shades: Intersectional Accuracy Disparities in Commercial Gender Classification. Proceedings of Machine Learning Research (PMLR), 81:1–15.

Methodology

Intersectional error analysis evaluating false classifications across the cross-product of gender and phenotypic skin type (Fitzpatrick scale categories I–VI grouped into lighter and darker). The audit demonstrates that assessing a system solely on aggregate accuracy (e.g., >90%) fails to capture severe disparities where particular subgroups experience error rates up to 34%.
